Bihar Geography — Original Notes

Location & extent

  • Landlocked state in eastern India; part of the Indo-Gangetic plain.
  • Borders: Nepal (north), West Bengal (east), Uttar Pradesh (west), Jharkhand (south).
  • 38 districts, 9 divisions. Capital: Patna (ancient Pataliputra).

Physical divisions

  • North Bihar Plain — north of the Ganga; fertile alluvial soil; flood-prone (Kosi, Gandak,

Bagmati). Terai belt along the Nepal border.

  • South Bihar Plain — south of the Ganga; narrower; drained by the Son, Punpun, Falgu;

relatively less flood-prone, more drought-prone in parts.

  • The Ganga bisects the state roughly east–west — the single most important geographical fact

about Bihar.

Climate & soils

  • Humid subtropical; hot summers, monsoon (June–September) brings most rainfall; cool winters.
  • Alluvial soil dominates — among the most fertile in India; supports intensive rice–wheat

cultivation.

  • Agro-climatic zones (3): North-West Alluvial Plain, North-East Alluvial Plain, South Bihar

Alluvial Plain.

Agriculture

  • Main crops: rice, wheat, maize, pulses, oilseeds, sugarcane.
  • Litchi — Bihar is India's #1 producer (Muzaffarpur belt; Shahi Litchi has a GI tag).
  • Makhana (foxnut) — Bihar is India's #1 producer (Mithila/Madhubani region; GI-tagged).
  • Mango (Bhagalpuri Zardalu — GI-tagged), banana, vegetables.
  • Floods (north) and waterlogging are the biggest agricultural constraints; the state pushes

flood-resilient and short-duration crop varieties.

Minerals & industry

  • After Jharkhand's separation (2000), Bihar retained few mineral reserves — mostly limestone,

mica (Gaya–Nawada belt), and minor minerals.

  • Industry: agro-processing (sugar mills, rice mills), handloom/silk (Bhagalpur silk —

GI-tagged), food processing; the state is pushing industrial corridors and ethanol units.

  • Energy: largely dependent on central allocation; thermal plants at Barh, Nabinagar, Buxar.

Disasters

  • Floods — north Bihar's annual crisis (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati); embankments + the Kosi

barrage (India–Nepal) are key infrastructure.

  • Drought/delayed monsoon in south Bihar; heatwaves in summer.

Forests & wildlife

  • Forest cover is low (~7–8%); Valmiki Tiger Reserve (West Champaran) — Bihar's only tiger

reserve, on the Nepal border.

  • Other protected spots: Bhimbandh, Gautam Buddha, Kaimur wildlife sanctuaries; Kanwar Lake

(Begusarai) — important wetland/bird area.

Map-work checklist (practice weekly)

Mark on an outline map: Ganga's entry/exit, Kosi, Gandak, Son, Bagmati; Patna, Gaya, Bhagalpur, Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, Purnia, Sonepur; Valmiki TR; Nepal border districts (West/East Champaran, Sitamarhi, Madhubani, Supaul, Araria, Kishanganj).
