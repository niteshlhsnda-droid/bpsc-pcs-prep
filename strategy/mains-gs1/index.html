Mains GS-I Topic Guides (300 marks)

GS-I covers History, the National Movement, Geography, and Polity & Governance, with a heavy Bihar current running through all four. Roughly a quarter of the paper is Bihar-specific — and Bihar content is where average answers become topper answers. Use the answer-writing skeleton for every outline below.

1. History (~25–30%) — think in continuities

Ancient: IVC → Vedic → Mahajanapadas (Magadh rises) → Mauryas (Pataliputra, Ashoka) → Guptas (Nalanda under Kumaragupta I) → Palas (Vikramshila under Dharmapala). Medieval: Sultanate → Mughals → Sher Shah (Sasaram, Bihar) → Bhakti–Sufi. Modern: Plassey 1757 → Buxar 1764 → Diwani 1765 → 1857 (Kunwar Singh) → Congress phases → Champaran 1917 → Quit India → 1947 → post-independence consolidation.

How to answer history: anchor the question on the timeline, show what came before (causes) and what it caused (consequences). Examiners reward continuity thinking — e.g. Buxar 1764 → Diwani rights 1765 → the Company becomes a state.

Original model outline — art & culture: "Discuss the contribution of Bihar to ancient Indian education."

  • Intro: Nalanda (5th c., Kumaragupta I) and Vikramshila (8th c., Dharmapala) — the world's

first residential universities, in present-day Bihar.

  • Body: curriculum (Buddhist philosophy, logic, medicine, astronomy), international scholars

(Xuanzang, Yijing), library scale, state patronage model; decline (12th–13th c. invasions).

  • Visual: timeline marking both universities; map dot for Nalanda/Rajgir/Antichak.
  • Conclusion: link to the modern Nalanda University revival — continuity from ancient to

aspirational Bihar.

2. Indian National Movement (~10–15%)

Early nationalism → extremist phase → Gandhian phase (Champaran 1917 is BPSC's favourite) → revolutionary movement → Quit India 1942 (Bihar's role was prominent) → independence. For every movement, learn: cause → method → leaders (with Bihar names) → outcome → significance.

Original model outline: "Champaran was Gandhi's laboratory. Discuss."

  • Intro: 1917, indigo cultivators, tinkathia — Gandhi's first Indian satyagraha.
  • Body: method tested (investigation + non-violent defiance); leaders incubated

(Rajendra Prasad, Kripalani); concessions won (1918 Agrarian Act); template set for Kheda, Ahmedabad, then the all-India movements.

  • Conclusion: Champaran proved the method; the method then made the movement.

3. Geography (~20–25%) — draw, don't describe

Physical, human and economic geography of India, then Bihar geography: North vs South Bihar plains, river systems, floods, agriculture, districts, disaster management.

River-system rule for answers: Himalayan rivers (perennial, flood-prone in north Bihar) vs peninsular/southern rivers (seasonal). Name five north-bank and three south-bank rivers from the schematic above — that one sketch answers half the Bihar-geography questions.

Monsoon answers: always name the mechanism (differential heating → low pressure over land), the two branches (Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal), and Bihar's position (Bay of Bengal branch funnels up the Ganga plain). Add the exam keywords: ITCZ shift, onset ~1 June (Kerala), retreat from October.

Original model outline — disaster management: "Suggest a flood-management strategy for north Bihar." → use the flood mind-map on the GS-II page: causes → effects → three-layer mitigation (structural, non-structural, Nepal cooperation), concluding with "living with floods" adaptation.

4. Polity & Governance (~20–25%)

Constitution-making, Preamble, FRs (14–32) / DPSP / Fundamental Duties, Union and State executive-legislature, judiciary (Patna High Court), Centre–State relations, landmark amendments (42nd, 44th, 73rd, 74th, 101st), Panchayati Raj.

Panchayat answers: draw the three tiers (Zila Parishad → Panchayat Samiti → Gram Panchayat) on the Gram Sabha foundation, then land the Bihar edge — 50% reservation for women, first state in India — plus the 73rd amendment backbone. That single Bihar fact, placed in the body, is worth more than a paragraph of generic theory.

Original model outline: "Evaluate the working of Panchayati Raj in Bihar."

  • Intro: 73rd amendment (1992) + Bihar Panchayat Raj Act; Bihar's 50% women's reservation.
  • Body — achievements: women's political participation, local planning, MGNREGA delivery;

challenges: fund-function-functionary mismatch, bureaucratic capture, capacity gaps.

  • Visual: the three-tier pyramid.
  • Conclusion: "democratic deepening is real but incomplete" — devolve the 3Fs.
