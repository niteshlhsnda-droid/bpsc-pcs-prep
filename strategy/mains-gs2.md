# Mains GS-II Topic Guides (300 marks)

GS-II is the most scoring paper for Bihar-prepared candidates: Economy, Science & Technology,
Environment — and **Bihar Special at 30–40%**, the single largest block in the whole Mains.
The rule of the paper: every answer gets a Bihar example, every process gets a flowchart,
every number gets a source.

## 1. Indian Economy (~25–30%) — concepts first, Bihar always

Planning era → 1991 LPG reforms → NITI Aayog; agriculture & food security; industry
(MSME, PLI); banking & RBI; fiscal policy (Budget, GST); poverty & employment.

![Three approaches to measuring GDP](assets/svg/gdp-approaches.svg)

**GDP answers:** state the three equivalent approaches (production, expenditure C+I+G+NX,
income), give India's sector shares, then land the Bihar twist — Bihar's GVA is
agriculture-heavy, so monsoon and floods are *GDP* news here, not just agriculture news.

![Demand-pull vs cost-push inflation](assets/svg/inflation-types.svg)

**Inflation answers:** name the type (demand-pull vs cost-push), give one mechanism, one
Bihar link (diesel → farm costs; MSP → rural demand), then the correct cure — rate hikes
fix demand-pull, supply-side action fixes cost-push. Examiners check whether you match
the cure to the cause.

![Union Budget process flowchart](assets/svg/budget-cycle.svg)

**Fiscal answers:** draw the budget process in 90 seconds — presented 1 February (Art. 112) →
general discussion → committee scrutiny → grants vote (Lok Sabha only, cut motions) →
Appropriation Bill (Art. 114) → Finance Bill. Pair with the bill-to-law flowchart for
legislative questions.

**Original model outline:** *"Discuss the challenges of agricultural marketing in Bihar
and suggest reforms."*
- Intro: agriculture-heavy GVA, smallholder-dominated, flood/monsoon vulnerability.
- Body: challenges — APMC gaps, storage/cold-chain deficit, middlemen margins, MSP
  awareness low; reforms — e-NAM, FPOs, warehousing (with Bihar's makhana/litchi as
  value-chain examples), crop insurance.
- Visual: a simple value-chain flow — farmer → FPO → storage → market → consumer,
  marking where margins leak.
- Conclusion: from production-centric to income-centric policy (double farmer income lens).

## 2. Science & Technology (~15–20%) — static + current hook

Space (ISRO — Chandrayaan, Gaganyaan, Aditya-L1), defence tech, IT/AI, biotech, health,
energy. Method: learn the **static principle** (e.g. how a launch vehicle stages),
then attach **one current mission** as the hook. BPSC asks the principle through the news.

## 3. Environment & Ecology (~10%) — laws + Bihar sites

Biodiversity, climate change, pollution, protected areas, environmental laws
(Environment Protection Act 1986, Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Forest Conservation Act).
**Bihar anchors:** Valmiki Tiger Reserve (only tiger reserve), Vikramshila Gangetic
Dolphin Sanctuary (Bhagalpur), Kanwar Lake (Asia's large freshwater oxbow lake),
Ganga dolphin as National Aquatic Animal.

## 4. Bihar Special (~30–40%) — the paper's core

Culture (Chhath, Sonepur Mela, Madhubani art), personalities (Chanakya, Dr. Rajendra Prasad,
JP, Dinkar), education, economy & schemes (**Saat Nischay 2.0**, Student Credit Card,
**Kanya Utthan**), demographics & districts, development issues (floods, migration).

![Floods in Bihar: causes, effects and mitigation mind-map](assets/svg/flood-cycle.svg)

**The flood answer — the single most repeated Bihar question.** Draw the mind-map:
causes (Himalayan silt-heavy rivers, concentrated monsoon, embankment paradox, Nepal
catchment) → effects (kharif loss, migration, disease) → three-layer mitigation
(structural, non-structural, Nepal cooperation). Conclude with "living with floods" —
adaptation beats resistance.

**Original model outline:** *"Migration from Bihar: causes, consequences and policy response."*
- Intro: Bihar among India's largest source states for out-migration; remittance economy.
- Body — causes: agrarian distress + floods, limited industrialisation, education-seeking;
  consequences: remittance inflow vs demographic/skilled-labour drain, urban pressure at
  destinations, left-behind families; policy: skill missions, industrial incentives,
  MGNREGA strengthening, destination-state portability of welfare.
- Conclusion: convert distress migration into *choice* migration through local jobs —
  cite the state's industrial policy push as the way forward.

**Original model outline:** *"Evaluate a flagship Bihar scheme (e.g. Mukhyamantri Kanya
Utthan Yojana)."*
- Intro: what the scheme is, whom it covers, lifecycle approach (birth → education → graduation).
- Body: design strengths (conditional cash transfers, girl-child focus); implementation
  gaps (awareness, DBT delays, school retention linkages); outcomes to cite (enrolment data).
- Conclusion: lifecycle cash-plus-services is the right model — deepen delivery, measure outcomes.

## 5. Answer discipline for GS-II

- **One Bihar example per answer**, minimum — schemes, districts, data.
- **One visual per answer**, maximum — flowchart, chart, or mind-map.
- Underline articles (110, 112, 114), scheme names, and data points.
