# Prelims vs Mains — One Preparation, Two Skills

The biggest planning mistake in BPSC prep is treating Prelims and Mains as two separate
exams. They are one syllabus tested with two different skills. Prepare once, revise twice.

![Venn diagram: Prelims vs Mains overlap](assets/svg/prelims-mains-venn.svg)

## Head-to-head

| | Prelims | Mains |
|---|---|---|
| Format | 150 MCQs, 2 hours | Descriptive papers, 3 hours each |
| Marks | 150 (qualifying only) | 1050 + 120 interview = merit |
| Negative marking | ⅓ per wrong answer | None |
| Pace | ~48 seconds per question | 8–10 min per answer |
| Tests | Recognition of facts | Analysis, structure, expression |
| Bihar weight | ~20–25% | ~30–40% of GS-II alone |

## The overlap — study these once, use them twice

- **Same NCERT base** for history, geography, polity, economy, science.
- **Same current affairs** — Prelims asks the fact, Mains asks the analysis of the same event.
- **Same Bihar facts** — rivers, schemes, firsts, personalities appear in both.
- **Same PYQ themes** — a Prelims MCQ theme this year is a Mains question next year.

## What needs separate preparation

**Prelims-only skills:** elimination technique, 48-second pacing, educated guessing under
negative marking, rapid-fire factual recall. Train with timed mocks — see the
[Prelims blueprint](../syllabus/prelims-blueprint.md).

**Mains-only skills:** the [intro–body–conclusion discipline](strategy/mains-answer-writing.md),
hand-drawn diagrams (flowcharts, maps, timelines), data + article + scheme value-addition,
Bihar examples in every GS-II answer, 3-hour writing stamina. Train with daily timed
answer writing — see [what toppers do](strategy/topper-methods.md).

## The integrated weekly rhythm

| Phase | Prelims share | Mains share |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1–8 | 30% (facts, MCQs) | 70% (notes, answer writing) |
| Months 9–10 | 50 / 50 | 50 / 50 |
| Last 60 days before Prelims | 90% | 10% (maintain writing hand) |
| Prelims → Mains gap | 10% | 90% |

**The rule:** learn the fact first (Prelims depth), then learn to argue it (Mains depth).
A topic isn't "done" until you can both tick its MCQ and write 200 words on it with a diagram.
