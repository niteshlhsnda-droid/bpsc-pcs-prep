# Mains Answer-Writing Frameworks

Mains is not a knowledge test — it is a **writing** test. Two candidates with the same facts
score 20 marks apart on presentation alone. This page gives you the reusable skeleton,
the examiner's checklist, and three fully worked model-answer outlines (history, polity,
geography) with the diagrams to draw in the exam hall.

## The IBC skeleton — every answer, every time

![The IBC answer skeleton: decode demand, intro, body, visual, conclusion](assets/svg/answer-skeleton.svg)

**Decode the demand first.** The verb tells you the shape:
- **Discuss** → present multiple sides, end balanced.
- **Analyse** → break into parts, show causes/effects, weigh them.
- **Critically examine / evaluate** → strengths AND weaknesses, then your verdict.
- **Comment** → brief take + justification; shorter than "discuss".

## The examiner's checklist (what actually earns marks)

1. **Structure on sight:** intro–body–conclusion, subheadings, underlined keywords.
2. **Demand addressed:** every directive word answered; no drifting.
3. **Value addition:** one article number, one judgment/scheme, one data point per answer.
4. **Bihar connect:** especially in GS-II — examiners reward state context (topper consensus).
5. **One visual:** a flowchart, map, timeline or table where it fits — never more than one per answer.
6. **Balanced tone:** optimistic, constitutional, solution-oriented conclusions.

## Time & word budget (3-hour paper)

| Paper | Marks | Questions (typical) | Per answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| GS-I / GS-II | 300 | ~15–20 | 8–10 min, 150–200 words |
| Essay | 150 | 1–2 essays | ~90 min each, 1000–1200 words |
| Hindi (qualifying) | 100 | mixed | don't over-invest; just clear 30% |

Practice rule: **write 2–3 answers daily under timed conditions** from the start of Mains prep,
and full 3-hour papers in the last two months. Speed is trained, not wished for.

## Model outline 1 — GS-I History

**Question type:** *"Discuss the significance of the Champaran Satyagraha (1917) in the Indian
freedom struggle."*

**Outline:**
- **Intro (2 lines):** Champaran, Bihar, 1917 — Gandhi's first satyagraha in India, launched
  against the tinkathia system imposed on indigo cultivators.
- **Body — why it mattered:**
  - *Method:* first application of satyagraha on Indian soil — template for later movements.
  - *Leadership:* transformed Gandhi from a South-Africa returnee into an all-India leader;
    brought Rajendra Prasad, J.B. Kripalani into the national movement.
  - *Outcome:* abolition of tinkathia (1918 Champaran Agrarian Act); proved non-violent
    mass action could extract concessions.
  - *Bihar anchor:* first of Bihar's three landmark contributions — Champaran 1917,
    Quit India 1942, JP Movement 1974.
- **Visual:** draw the one-line timeline, mark 1917, show "before (moderate petitions) →
  after (mass satyagraha era)".

![Indian history one-line timeline with Bihar anchors](assets/svg/history-timeline.svg)

- **Conclusion:** Champaran proved the method; the method then made the movement. End on the
  continuity point — examiners reward "what it caused", not just "what it was".

## Model outline 2 — GS-II Polity

**Question type:** *"Explain the legislative procedure for a Money Bill. How does it differ
from an ordinary bill?"*

**Outline:**
- **Intro:** Article 110 defines a Money Bill; Article 107–111 govern ordinary bills. The
  difference exists because taxation is the Lok Sabha's democratic privilege.
- **Body — the flow:** introduction → Lok Sabha (3 readings) → Rajya Sabha (14 days,
  recommendations only) → President (must assent, cannot return) → Act.
- **Contrast table:** ordinary bill — either House, joint sitting possible (Art. 108),
  President may return once; Money Bill — Lok Sabha only, Speaker certifies, no joint sitting.
- **Visual:** the bill-to-law flowchart (draw in ~90 seconds).

![Flowchart: how a bill becomes law](assets/svg/lawmaking-flow.svg)

- **Conclusion:** the asymmetry is deliberate — the directly elected House controls the purse.
  Link to fiscal accountability: no taxation without representation.

## Model outline 3 — GS-I/GS-II Geography (Bihar)

**Question type:** *"Bihar is India's most flood-prone state. Discuss causes and suggest
a mitigation strategy."*

**Outline:**
- **Intro:** ~73% of Bihar's area is flood-prone; the Kosi's shifting course earned it the
  title "Sorrow of Bihar".
- **Body:** use the mind-map — causes (Himalayan rivers + silt, concentrated monsoon,
  embankment paradox, Nepal catchment), effects (kharif loss, migration, disease),
  mitigation in three layers (structural, non-structural, cooperative with Nepal).
- **Visual:** the rivers-of-Bihar schematic (2-minute draw) or the flood mind-map.

![Schematic map of Bihar's rivers](assets/svg/bihar-rivers.svg)

- **Conclusion:** shift from "fighting floods" to "living with floods" — adaptation,
  early warning, insurance (link to the mitigation hierarchy).

## The daily drill

1. Pick 2–3 questions from previous-year themes (never write blind — write to a demand).
2. Set a timer (8–10 min each). Draw the visual, don't describe it.
3. Self-review against the six-point checklist above; underline what you missed.
