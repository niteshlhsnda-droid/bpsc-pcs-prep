BPSC Mains Syllabus (CCE, 71st pattern)

Five papers, 3 hours each, all descriptive except where noted. Final merit = 1050 (mains) + 120 (interview) = 1170. There is no negative marking in Mains.

Pattern verified from the 71st CCE notification (Sep 2026). The 70th CCE used a different structure — always confirm against the latest official notification before you plan.

Paper 1 — General Hindi (100 marks, qualifying)

Need 30% (30 marks) to qualify; marks are not counted in the merit.

SectionMarksWhat it tests
Nibandh (essay)30300–400 words on social/cultural/economic topics
Vyakaran (grammar)30Sandhi, samas, vilom, paryayvachi, muhavare, lokoktiyan, vakya-shuddhi
Apathit gadyansh20Unseen passage + comprehension questions
Anuvad / sankshipt lekhan20English→Hindi translation / precis writing

Strategy: don't ignore it — failing Hindi ends your attempt regardless of GS scores. One hour a week of grammar + one essay a fortnight is enough for most Hindi-background candidates.

Paper 2 — General Studies I (300 marks, merit)

  • History (~25–30%): ancient (civilizations to post-Gupta), medieval (Sultanate, Mughals,

Bhakti–Sufi), modern (British rule to post-independence), art & culture, Bihar history (Magadh, Nalanda, Champaran, JP Movement)

  • Indian National Movement (~10–15%): early nationalism, extremist phase, Gandhian phase

(Champaran 1917), revolutionary movement, post-independence consolidation

  • Geography (~20–25%): physical, human & economic geography; Bihar geography (plains,

rivers, floods, agriculture, districts); disaster management

  • Polity & Governance (~20–25%): Constitution, rights & duties, Union/State/local government,

judiciary, Bihar governance (Vidhan Sabha, Vidhan Parishad, state administration)

  • National current affairs (~10–15%)

Paper 3 — General Studies II (300 marks, merit)

  • Indian Economy (~25–30%): planning & reforms, agriculture, industry, banking & finance,

fiscal policy, trade, poverty & employment; Bihar economy (GSDP, schemes, state budget)

  • Science & Technology (~15–20%): space, defence, IT/AI, biotechnology, health, energy
  • Environment & Ecology (~10%): biodiversity, climate change, pollution, protected areas,

environmental laws

  • Bihar Special (~30–40%) — the most scoring section: culture (Chhath, Sonepur Mela,

Madhubani art), personalities (Chanakya, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, JP, Dinkar), education, economy & schemes (Saat Nischay 2.0, Student Credit Card, Kanya Utthan), demographics & districts, development issues (floods, migration)

  • State-level current affairs (~10%)

Paper 4 — Optional Subject (300 marks, merit)

One optional from the BPSC list, descriptive. Choose by graduation subject + interest + GS overlap, not by rumours of "scoring" optionals.

OptionalGS overlapNotes
HistoryVery highNatural fit with GS-I
GeographyHighFits GS-I geography
Public AdministrationModerateHelps GS-II governance
SociologyLow–moderateCompact, answer-friendly
Hindi LiteratureLowHigh scoring if you love literature
PhilosophyLowShort syllabus, logical

Decide your optional in the first month. 300 marks is too big to leave to chance.

Paper 5 — Essay / Nibandh (150 marks, merit)

Essays across categories: social issues, Bihar-specific topics, economy, governance, science & tech, international affairs, philosophical quotes.

Essay method: 3-part structure (intro with hook → 4–5 dimensioned body paragraphs → balanced conclusion), Bihar examples wherever relevant, one quote or data point per essay. Write one full essay every week from month 4 onward.

Mains answer-writing rules

  1. Intro–body–conclusion in every answer; underline key terms.
  2. Bihar examples in every GS-II answer — examiners reward state context.
  3. Maps, flowcharts, and tables beat paragraphs for geography/economy answers.
  4. Word/time discipline: practice full 3-hour papers, not just single answers.
